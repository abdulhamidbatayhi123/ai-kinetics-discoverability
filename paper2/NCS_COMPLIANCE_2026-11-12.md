## 2. Editorials: what NCS wants or rejects, and whether the manuscript answers it

### 2.1 "To review or not to review" (Nat. Comput. Sci. 1, 241; 26 Apr 2021), https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-021-00068-1
- NCS is "mostly interested in the development of new computational/mathematical methods"; applications of existing methods "should normally come with methodological development".
  Bears directly: this study develops no method. Answer: submit as **Analysis** (section 3), whose definition fits exactly.
- "the authors must demonstrate the paper's potential impact by performing experiments with real data and by benchmarking against the most relevant related works". Benchmarking: answered (six sparse methods, symbolic regression, a neural pipeline, KAN exploratory). Real data: **not answered**; the only real-data content is order-of-magnitude Da estimates of published columns (Supplementary Table 1) and the Santana et al. anchor point.
- Preference for papers "with potential impact across multiple disciplines". The cross-disciplinary mechanism (an error in a known constitutive input biases the coefficient ratio discovery must recover, while a one-parameter fit absorbs it) is argued in the cover letter but only for the isotherm in the Discussion. Answer: one Discussion sentence naming the general class (any discovery problem with an imperfectly known constitutive input or closure, e.g. reaction rate laws with uncertain equilibrium constants), without overclaiming.

### 2.2 "Experimental validation, anyone?" (3, 361; 25 May 2023), https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-023-00462-x
- NCS "may" ask for validation with real experimental data; "err on the side of including an experimental validation when feasible and appropriate"; existing public data are an accepted route.
  Not answered. Two options, in order of value:
  (a) An exploratory (post hoc, declared before running) test on one published experimental breakthrough dataset at outlet resolution (O3), e.g. the Dantas et al. 13X runs already used for Supplementary Table 1, checking the map's prediction (discovery fails, k identifiable within the factor-of-two criterion or not). Only if the raw curves are obtainable at adequate quality; figure-digitised curves would need to be labelled as such.
  (b) At minimum, a sentence in the Introduction or Discussion stating why simulation is the instrument: scoring discovery requires a known true law, which real columns cannot supply. Put the same argument in the cover letter.

### 2.3 "Formats for reporting primary research" (3, 993; 18 Dec 2023), https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-023-00588-y
- Article: "substantial computational developments and extensive benchmarking and validations ... the computational developments must be conceptually novel".
- **Analysis: "systematic comparisons of computational methods and tools of high importance for a field ... leading to important practical and arresting conclusions about their performances. An Analysis should provide a quantitative performance evaluation of methods or tools at their best, and generally should not describe the development of a new method or tool."** The manuscript (methods run "in the strongest configuration we could verify") is this format. Editors may switch formats themselves, but choosing Analysis removes the "no methodological development" objection at triage.

### 2.4 "What reviewers request the most" (6, 317; 28 Apr 2026), https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-026-00989-9
- Benchmarks must include state-of-the-art or recent methods; justify every method choice, configuration and exclusion. Partly answered (Methods "Discovery methods"; cost rules for SR subgrid; GPU-scale UDE not run). Check that very recent discovery methods are at least discussed (e.g. NCS's own 2025 symbolic-regression papers: PSE, s43588-025-00904-8; ND2, s43588-025-00893-8; BILLIE, s43588-025-00804-x) with a reason for exclusion; reviewers chosen by NCS are likely to know them.
- No overselling ("first of its kind", "paradigm shift"). Answered: text uses "to our knowledge" once and modest claims.
- Limitations must be discussed. Answered (Discussion limitations paragraph).
- Selective reporting and statistics. Answered strongly (pre-registration, as-run and corrected verdicts, ED Fig. 8, SI Note 3, bootstrap intervals). A reviewer may still ask how the date of the freeze is evidenced independently of git history, since the history was moved between repositories (Methods says so). State in the cover letter where the frozen plan was publicly visible on 11 October 2026 (GitHub push or Zenodo record date), if such evidence exists.
- Clarity of figures and methods. The dense Methods (12 subsections, about 3,000 words) are acceptable; NCS imposes no Methods limit.

## 4. Differences and recommendations (only where they should raise acceptance odds)

1. **Format: submit as Analysis, not Article.** Same limits; the Analysis definition (systematic comparison of methods "at their best", no new method) matches the study and pre-empts the 2021 triage criterion that applications of existing methods need methodological development.
2. **Real data.** The 2021 and 2023 editorials make real-data validation the largest triage risk for a simulation-only study. If a published breakthrough dataset with raw curves can be obtained, add one declared exploratory test (O3) in an Extended Data figure; otherwise state in the paper and the cover letter why ground truth requires simulation and what the map predicts for real columns (already partly in the Discussion).
3. **Introduction opening.** Lead with the general question for scientific ML (when can equation discovery recover a law that a fit can calibrate?) and move the fixed-bed description to the second paragraph. This matches every comparator and the 2025 accessibility editorial.
4. **Generality sentence.** In the Discussion, one sentence on the class of problems the regressor-error mechanism covers, phrased as a hypothesis, not a result.
5. **Title (optional).** Current: 13 words, declarative, keywords present. "Unidentifiable" is jargon for a general reader. A plainer option that keeps the claim: "Equation discovery fails long before parameter estimation in simulated adsorption columns" (11 words); check that "parameter estimation fails" is an accurate shorthand for the factor-of-two identifiability criterion before adopting it.
6. **Abstract.** Structure already matches. Consider (a) a "Here we" signal sentence, (b) replacing the last sentence with the community-level implication (calibrate an assumed law vs. discover it), and (c) defining or dropping "probes inside the bed". Keep <= 150 words.
7. **Fig. 6-style guidance.** The Discussion's three-step order of operations (discover, choose by likelihood, assume) is the paper's practical output; Analysis papers often give it a display item. The paper has one free display slot (5 of 6 used): a small decision map would fit, if it adds no new numbers beyond the results files.
8. **Source Data.** Export one Excel file per main figure (and Extended Data) from `results/p2_figdata.json` / `*.prov.json`; mention "Source data are provided with this paper" in Data availability.
9. **Code-review readiness.** README: install time, run time per step, a small demo with expected output, and a minutes-long path from shipped results to verdicts, Table 1 and figures. Fill the code and software submission checklist when asked. Fix README line 134 ("Supplementary Table 2" should be 1).
10. **Methods.** Optional "Statistics and reproducibility" heading (bootstrap intervals, replicate counts, seeding), as in Hummer et al. Add wall time per method (one sentence or SI table).
11. **AI statement.** Name the exact model identifiers and the verification of AI-assisted code.
12. **Cover letter.** Cut the abstract restatement; add Analysis-format rationale, why simulation, physics-informed ML fit, code quick path, preprint DOI and licence, and evidence for the freeze date; name recent NCS discovery papers only to position the work, never as blunt comparisons.
13. **Benchmark coverage.** Discuss (or justify excluding) the 2025 NCS discovery methods (PSE, ND2, BILLIE) in the Introduction or Methods; NCS reviewers are likely drawn from these groups.
14. **AIP-stage only:** remove personal macros, paste references into the .tex, Nature reference style, drop `lineno`/`xurl`, trim method detail from legends, resolve colour-in-print question.
